### Jakob's Law {#jakob-s-law}

- {#users-will-transfer-expectations-they-have-built-around-one-familiar} Users will transfer expectations they have built around one familiar product to another that appears similar.
- {#by-leveraging-existing-mental-models-we-can-create-superior-user} By leveraging existing mental models, we can create superior user experiences in which the users can focus on their tasks rather than on learning new models.
- {#when-making-changes-minimize-discord-by-empowering-users-to-continue} When making changes, minimize discord by empowering users to continue using a familiar version for a limited time.

### Fitts’s Law {#fitts-s-law}

- {#touch-targets-should-be-large-enough-for-users-to-accurately} Touch targets should be large enough for users to accurately select them.
- {#touch-targets-should-have-ample-spacing-between-them} Touch targets should have ample spacing between them.
- {#touch-targets-should-be-placed-in-areas-of-an-interface} Touch targets should be placed in areas of an interface that allow them to be easily acquired.

### Hick’s Law {#hick-s-law}

- {#minimize-choices-when-response-times-are-critical-to-increase-decision} Minimize choices when response times are critical to increase decision time.
- {#break-complex-tasks-into-smaller-steps-in-order-to-decrease} Break complex tasks into smaller steps in order to decrease cognitive load.
- {#avoid-overwhelming-users-by-highlighting-recommended-options} Avoid overwhelming users by highlighting recommended options.
- {#use-progressive-onboarding-to-minimize-cognitive-load-for-new-users} Use progressive onboarding to minimize cognitive load for new users.
- {#be-careful-not-to-simplify-to-the-point-of-abstraction} Be careful not to simplify to the point of abstraction.

### Miller’s Law {#miller-s-law}

- {#don-t-use-the-magical-number-seven-to-justify-unnecessary} Don’t use the “magical number seven” to justify unnecessary design limitations.
- {#organize-content-into-smaller-chunks-to-help-users-process-understand} Organize content into smaller chunks to help users process, understand, and memorize easily.
- {#remember-that-short-term-memory-capacity-will-vary-per-individual} Remember that short-term memory capacity will vary per individual, based on their prior knowledge and situational context.

### Postel’s Law {#postel-s-law}

- {#be-empathetic-to-flexible-about-and-tolerant-of-any-of} Be empathetic to, flexible about, and tolerant of any of the various actions the user could take or any input they might provide.
- {#anticipate-virtually-anything-in-terms-of-input-access-and-capability} Anticipate virtually anything in terms of input, access, and capability while providing a reliable and accessible interface.
- {#the-more-we-can-anticipate-and-plan-for-in-design} The more we can anticipate and plan for in design, the more resilient the design will be.
- {#accept-variable-input-from-users-translating-that-input-to-meet} Accept variable input from users, translating that input to meet your requirements, defining boundaries for input, and providing clear feedback to the user.

### Peak–End Rule {#peak-end-rule}

- {#pay-close-attention-to-the-most-intense-points-and-the} Pay close attention to the most intense points and the final moments (the “end”) of the user journey.
- {#identify-the-moments-when-your-product-is-most-helpful-valuable} Identify the moments when your product is most helpful, valuable, or entertaining and design to delight the end user.
- {#remember-that-people-recall-negative-experiences-more-vividly-than-positive} Remember that people recall negative experiences more vividly than positive ones.

### Aesthetic–Usability Effect {#aesthetic-usability-effect}

- {#an-aesthetically-pleasing-design-creates-a-positive-response-in-people} An aesthetically pleasing design creates a positive response in people’s brains and leads them to believe the design actually works better.
- {#people-are-more-tolerant-of-minor-usability-issues-when-the} People are more tolerant of minor usability issues when the design of a product or service is aesthetically pleasing.
- {#visually-pleasing-design-can-mask-usability-problems-and-prevent-issues} Visually pleasing design can mask usability problems and prevent issues from being discovered during usability testing.

### von Restorff Effect {#von-restorff-effect}

- {#make-important-information-or-key-actions-visually-distinctive} Make important information or key actions visually distinctive.
- {#use-restraint-when-placing-emphasis-on-visual-elements-to-avoid} Use restraint when placing emphasis on visual elements to avoid them competing with one another and to ensure salient items don’t get mistakenly identified as ads.
- {#don-t-exclude-those-with-a-color-vision-deficiency-or} Don’t exclude those with a color vision deficiency or low vision by relying exclusively on color to communicate contrast.
- {#carefully-consider-users-with-motion-sensitivity-when-using-motion-to} Carefully consider users with motion sensitivity when using motion to communicate contrast.

### Tesler’s Law {#tesler-s-law}

- {#all-processes-have-a-core-of-complexity-that-cannot-be} All processes have a core of complexity that cannot be designed away and therefore must be assumed by either the system or the user.
- {#ensure-as-much-as-possible-of-the-burden-is-lifted} Ensure as much as possible of the  burden is lifted from users by dealing with inherent complexity during design and development.
- {#take-care-not-to-simplify-interfaces-to-the-point-of} Take care not to simplify interfaces to the point of abstraction.

### Doherty Threshold {#doherty-threshold}

- {#provide-system-feedback-within-400-ms-in-order-to-keep} Provide system feedback within 400 ms in order to keep users’ attention and increase productivity.
- {#use-perceived-performance-to-improve-response-time-and-reduce-the} Use perceived performance to improve response time and reduce the perception of waiting.
- {#animation-is-one-way-to-visually-engage-people-while-loading} Animation is one way to visually engage people while loading or processing is happening in the background.
- {#progress-bars-help-make-wait-times-tolerable-regardless-of-their} Progress bars help make wait times tolerable, regardless of their accuracy.
- {#purposefully-adding-a-delay-to-a-process-can-actually-increase} Purposefully adding a delay to a process can actually increase its perceived value and instill a sense of trust, even when the process itself actually takes much less time.
